## Consistency and limits

No material contradiction or missing policy guidance was found for these five requests. The entry point, authorization policy, review procedure, Git protocol, implementation checkpoints and campaign templates consistently separate reviewer completion at a report commit from coordinator/execution-owner publication; carry established authorization; honor explicit local limits; retain interrupted commits; and distinguish task completion from phase integration and hosted reconciliation.

The review statement that a read-only review does not authorize external effects is consistent with publication owned by the coordinating workflow under **separately established workflow authority**. A workflow label alone supplies neither authority nor destination. Likewise, revision integration wording is qualified by the coherent authorized boundary and main-phase gate; integrating a revision into a phase does not authorize merging an incomplete phase into main.

Campaign templates describe evidence/presentation, not authority. They distinguish planned, verified, committed, merged and published states and explicitly reject structural checks as proof of client/provider execution. This assessment predicts instructed behavior from source; it cannot prove that a platform permits execution, that authentication works, that a remote contains a commit, or that a provider operation succeeds. Those require observed execution/readback. No extra journal, authorization registry, preceding review, PR operation or approval gate is required by the inspected instructions.
